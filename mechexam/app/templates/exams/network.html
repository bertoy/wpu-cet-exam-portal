Setting up the room

Three ways to do it, from simplest to best. In all of them the portal runs on your laptop, not on the internet, so the exam keeps working whatever the campus connection does.

  1. A router with nothing plugged into its internet socket. Everyone joins it, your laptop runs the portal, and there is no way out to the internet for anybody — including you. The simplest to be sure of, because there is nothing to misconfigure: if the cable is out, the internet is out.
  2. A router with the internet blocked for students only. Most home routers can reserve an address for your laptop and apply parental controls or an access schedule to every other device. You keep the internet, the room does not. Check it from a student's phone before the exam rather than trusting the setting.
  3. Your phone as the access point, with mobile data off. Turn on the hotspot, turn mobile data off, and the hotspot becomes a local network with no uplink. Good for a small group and needs no hardware, but a phone hotspot usually caps how many devices may join — often eight or ten, so not a class of sixty.

Whichever you choose, fill in the allowed networks above as well. The router stops a phone reaching the internet; only the portal can stop a phone leaving the room's wifi altogether and reaching the exam over mobile data.

⚠

Two things to know before relying on this. A phone can be a hotspot for other phones, so one student with mobile data can share it with the row — the allowed-networks rule does not see that, because those phones still arrive on a wifi address. And if you run the exam on your laptop, the marks live on your laptop: take a backup from Administration ▸ System check afterwards and before anything else.
